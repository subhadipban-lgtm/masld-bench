The disease-specific, histology-grounded
omics-LLM benchmark.

MASLD-Bench evaluates large language models on metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease biology — biopsy-staged transcriptomics, blood and plasma cfRNA, perturbation transfer — under a frozen-corpus, pre-registered, paired-statistics discipline. It is the disease-specific replication and extension of the LongevityBench design, with the statistical rigor and honest negatives that mean-rank evaluations cannot express.

The five claims

C1 · Infrastructure

19 frozen corpora spanning liver transcriptomics with biopsy endpoints, DNA methylation, blood-proxy severity, liver→plasma proteomics, plasma cfRNA, and perturbation transfer — with SHA-256 manifests and documented corrections (never buried).

C2 · Compact vs frontier

Under paired testing, the 1.7B fine-tune wins fibrosis regression outright (MB-2005 MAE 1.226 vs the best frontier's 1.529) while tying on binary tasks — and the 8B adds little more. Scale is not the binding constraint.

C3 · The honest ledger

Novel task families with pre-registered classical gates: where a logistic regression reads signal (perturbation 0.833), LLMs sit at majority — zero-shot and trained-on. Documented negatives are results.

C4 · The representation-format regime split

Classical ML and LLMs occupy disjoint regimes on fixed-panel omics… except on plasma cfRNA (C5), where frontier models are statistically indistinguishable from the linear reader. The barrier is conditional on the data — and five genes carry the readable signal.

C5 · Label noise, quantified

The same predictions score 0.389 against reader A's biopsy golds and 0.259 against reader B's (κ = 0.384). Dual-reader staging variability is a measurable performance ceiling — this benchmark is the first to freeze both readers and report the gap.

Research use only. MASLD-Bench and its fine-tuned models are research artifacts — not diagnostic, prognostic, or clinical tools. Nothing here is validated for patient care.
